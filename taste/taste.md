# Taste

Taste is the durable judgment layer for this workspace. Owned by John Rubino. Agents inherit it; they do not invent around it.

## North star

Build product instruments for high-stakes work: wealth, clearing, servicing, and complex ops. Clear enough to trust in one glance, precise enough for repeated expert use, calm enough that the work stays primary. Systems thinking over screen theater.

## Product feel

- Dense, not crowded — information earns its seat; chrome does not.
- Cut, not fade — hierarchy comes from interruption and contrast, not soft gradients.
- Refined, not precious — craft shows in restraint and state coverage, not ornament.
- Systematic, not sterile — patterns repeat with purpose; paired structures beat centered hero layouts.
- Familiar by default; novel only when the benefit is visible on the job.

## Interface values

1. The first screen should be the real tool whenever possible.
2. One screen, one primary job. Secondary work yields or waits.
3. Core objects and their state should be visible and manipulable — especially money movement, account status, and risk.
4. Empty states create momentum toward the next real action, not copy that apologizes.
5. Typography is quiet, sharp, and readable at densities experts actually use.
6. Motion explains state or continuity. Never decorate uncertainty or loading with glow.
7. Color marks hierarchy, state, or meaning. A cool accent on a warm-neutral field is enough; purple AI gradients are banned.
8. Utility screens get the same craft as showcase screens — handoff and QA live here.
9. Accessibility, performance, loading, empty, error, focus, and reduced-motion are design requirements, not polish passes.
10. Specs beat vibes. Tokens, spacing, states, and edge cases are the deliverable.

## Critique questions

- What is the primary object and action?
- Can an expert predict the next state without reading the labels twice?
- Is hierarchy earned by information value, or by decorative weight?
- Which details feel generic AI UI rather than this product?
- Are loading, empty, error, and permission-denied designed — or missing?
- What can be removed without breaking the work?
- Would this survive design–dev–QA handoff without a meeting to “explain the vibe”?

## Avoid

- Centered hero + text-left / graphic-right defaults.
- Generic AI purple gradients, glows, and shape-primitives standing in for hierarchy.
- Oversized marketing typography inside product tools.
- Pill, card, and shadow overload.
- Explanatory chrome that repeats what the visual already says.
- Decorative containers and custom controls worse than the platform native.
- Treating a static mockup as final when interaction or state is the question.
- Inventing user quotes or research; synthesize evidence, don’t fabricate it.

## Handoff bar (for engineering)

Before a screen or flow is “done,” it has: layout and spacing, tokens (color, type, radius, elevation), component states including empty/loading/error, interaction and motion behavior, responsive behavior, and named edge cases. If something is expensive or infeasible, revise the spec — do not restate it louder.

## Personal additions

- Prefer generated imagery, texture, and real motion over gradient theater when a visual identity is required.
- When a reference is given (game, era, object, art movement), treat it as the primary constraint and push it further than feels safe.
- Ideas come as many shallow options first; refine only after John reacts.
- Taste feedback from John becomes a standing rule, not a one-screen patch.
